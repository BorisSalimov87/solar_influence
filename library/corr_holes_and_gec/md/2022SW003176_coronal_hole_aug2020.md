## Ionospheric Response to the Coronal Hole Activity of August 2020: A Global Multi-Instrumental Overview

##### Waqar Younas, Majid Khan, C. Amory-Mazaudier, and P. O. Amaechi

**1 1 2 3**

1 2 Department of Physics, Quaid-i-Azam University, Islamabad, Pakistan, Sorbonne Université, Ecole Polytechnique, Institut Polytechnique de Paris, Université Paris Saclay, Observatoire de Paris, CNRS, Laboratoire de Physique des Plasmas (LPP), Paris, France,3Cardiff School of Education and Social Policy, Cardiff Metropolitan University-Cyncoed Campus, Cardiff, UK

**Abstract** We have studied the ionospheric response to a coronal hole event of August 2020 using the data from global ionospheric maps, ground magnetometers and parameters from the instruments onboard SWARM and thermosphere, ionosphere, mesosphere energetics and dynamics satellites. The role of different physical drivers, responsible for observed ionospheric disturbance, has been identified. On the storm day (2 August), a steady southward directed interplanetary magnetic field *Bz* caused the penetration of magnetospheric convection electric field and positive storm effect in daytime sectors. As a result of prompt penetration electric field (PPEF) on 2 August, a polar-ward expansion of day-side ionospheric plasma. On 3 August, the signatures of both disturbance dynamo electric field (DDEF) caused by disturbed thermospheric winds and PPEF have been observed. The westward PPEF and eastward disturbance DDEF on the night-side caused a strong enhancement in ionospheric plasma parameters at the corresponding sectors. The effect of disturbed thermospheric winds and resultant electric field persisted till the end of 7 August. The large decrease in O/N₂ ratio at northern mid-latitudes which is a consequence of the seasonal impact resulted in the negative storm effects at corresponding latitudinal regions. This study has shown that although the storm of August 2020 was a minor one, the associated high speed solar wind streams emanating from a coronal hole resulted in drastic changes in ionospheric parameters including global electron content, in situ electron density and total electron content at the equatorial ionization anomaly and equatorial electric field.

#### Plain Language Summary Coronal holes are regions on the surface of sun with open magnetic

fields. Such regions are associated with high speed solar winds which can significantly disturb the quiet-time ionosphere. In this work we studied the ionospheric disturbance as generated by coronal hole activity of August 2020 using the data from ground and space borne instruments. We have identified the role of various physical drivers which have been active during this particular space weather events. The signatures of penetrating electric fields, disturbance dynamo, and composition changes are found in this activity. This study highlight that such coronal hole activities can also perturb the ionospheric parameters. Hence, the modeling community should also take into account the role of such drivers while forecasting the ionospheric parameters.

##### 1. Introduction

Solar coronal holes (SCHs) are regions of relatively cool and dense plasma than the other parts of sun's corona (Cranmer, 2009; Krieger et al., 1973). The SCHs are often associated with emission of high speed solar wind streams (HSSWs), which have speed ranging from 750 to 800  Km/s (B. T. Tsurutani et  al.,  1995,  2018). As HSSWs overtake the preceding slow solar wind, a region of compressed plasma is formed which is called the corotating interaction region (CIR) (Smith & Wolfe,  1976). When such structure strikes the Earth's magneto- sphere, the magnetic reconnection between the southward magnetic fields within the CIR and the Earth's magne- topause magnetic fields causes solar wind energy injection into the magnetosphere (Dungey, 1961; Tsurutani, Gonzalez, et al., 2006).

The large energy inputs during the CIR also produce disturbances in the magnetosphere, ionosphere, and thermo- sphere (Bowman, 1996; B. T. Tsurutani et al., 1995). The CIRs are source of geomagnetic disturbances during the solar minimum and declining phase when probability of coronal mass ejection (CME) is very low (Gonzalez et al., 1999; Tsurutani, Gonzalez, et al., 2006). The effect of HSSWs on the terrestrial ionosphere have been stud- ied by many authors using different instruments and data analysis techniques (Gulyaeva & Gulyaev, 2020; Lei

On the other hand, the changes in the ionosphere during a recovery phase are mainly governed by the ionospheric disturbance dynamo electric fields (DDEF) and thermospheric compositional changes (Blanc & Richmond, 1980; Fejer & Emmert, 2003; Fejer et al., 1983). The magnetic signature of DDEF is an anti-Sq variation at low-latitudes and is referred as disturbance dynamo (Ddyn) (Le Huy & Amory-Mazaudier, 2005). Fathy et al. (2014) presented the planetary signatures of DDEF during the coronal hole activity of April 2010 and found that DDEF reduces the amplitude of horizontal component of magnetic field at low-latitudes. Their results also emphasis the longitudi- nal dependence of Ddyn. Rodríguez-Zuluaga et  al.  (2016) investigated the difference response, of low-latitude ionosphere, to CME and HSSWs events. This study highlights the role of oscillatory *Bz* component of inter- planetary magnetic field (IMF) in the generation of PPEF and DDEF. The equatorial ionization anomaly (EIA) during the HSSWs, in comparison with CME, events can be well correlated with the DDEF (Rodríguez-Zuluaga et al., 2016). Younas, Amory-Mazaudier, et al. (2021) studied the differences in Ddyn during the CME and HSSWs generated storms. The amplitude of Ddyn during the HSSWs and CIRs is relatively small as compared to CME events, however it persists for a relatively long duration. The HSSWs during the recovery phase of a magnetic storm also cause significant changes in the thermospheric O/N₂ as reported by Younas, Khan, et al. (2021).

Despite the fact that various studies have been carried out to analyze the ionosphere response to different space weather activities, a detail investigation of an isolated HSSWs event, and the respective signatures of various physical drivers, especially during minor storm events, need more attention. Such events generally do not develop strong ring currents which is a key parameter to quantify the strength of geomagnetic storms. Given the drastic changes in the geospace environment, their effect clearly needs to be taken into consideration by the modeling community. This is all the more significant given the fact that the minor storm of August 2020 and the associated HSSWs drove significant longitudinal changes in ionospheric as well as perturbations in magnetic parameters. In fact, the investigated storm of August 2020 is not just an ordinary weak event. It is a purely HSSWs event, which is generated due to the coronal hole activity. Earlier case studies are mainly related to the moderate or intense geomagnetic storms that are mostly caused by CMEs. Here we have found that disturbed thermospheric winds, DDEF and PPEF can also become significant during such a HSSW and consequently causes disturbance in quiet time ionosphere.

This work thus, highlights the impact of the minor storm driven by HSSWs on the terrestrial ionosphere with the view of isolating the contribution of individual storm drivers as well as the signature of the associated magnetic perturbations. This is with the view of comparing the ionospheric variability during such event with those driven by CME and intense to moderate storms. To this effect, we analyzed global variations in ionospheric and magnetic parameters during a recent coronal hole activity which occurred during the declining phase and solar minimum (August 2020) using diverse ground and space borne measurements. Section 2 provides the data sets and method- ology employed to analyze the ionospheric changes induced by considered coronal hole activity. The results are described in Section 3, whereas Section 4 presents the discussion. Finally, we summarize the work in Section 5.

##### 4. Discussion

It is known that there are various physical phenomena associated with iono- spheric disturbances during the storm-time, for example, PPEF, DDEF, and thermospheric composition changes are major drivers of ionospheric varia- tions induced by space weather activities (Blanc & Richmond, 1980; Burns et al., 1995; Huang et al., 2007; Kikuchi et al., 2008; Maruyama et al., 2007; Younas, Khan, et al., 2021).

The magnetosphere-ionosphere system acts as a high pass filter (in frequency domain) to IEF and allows penetration of electric fields to equatorial iono- sphere under rapidly oscillatory conditions or southward directed IMF *Bz*. Furthermore, the efficiency of penetration is enhanced under southward *Bz* component of IMF as compared to northward directed case. Consequently, oscillatory *Bz* component of IMF can cause the penetration of PPEF to iono- sphere (Kelley, 1989; B. Tsurutani et al., 2004; B. T. Tsurutani et al., 2008; Vasyliunas, 1970, 1972). Koga et al. (2011) and Wei et al. (2008) reported that long duration fluctuations of IMF *Bz* during HSSWs lead to continuous penetration of PPEF to low-latitude ionosphere. The solar wind controls the IEF and resultants current flow between the Earth's magnetosphere and the solar wind. Hence, high speed solar wind is more effective in penetration of eastward (westward) electric fields on day-side (night-side). A sudden north- ward turning of IMF *Bz* from a steady southward scenario can also lead to penetration of magnetospheric electric fields as efficiently as during south- ward directed *Bz* periods (Manoj et al.,  2008; B. T. Tsurutani et al.,  2008). Figure 1b shows that there are three major episodes of southward directed, and subsequent northward turning of, IMF *Bz* on 2 and 3 August 2020. The Minima of the *Bz* during these episodes occurred at 13:00 and 21:00 UT on 2 August and at 02:40 UT on 3 August. The magnetic signatures of PPEF (DP2) also depict a strong penetrating electric field on 2 and 3 August 2020 with maximum effect observed at 16:00 UT on 03 August (Figure 7b).

The IMF *Bz* remain southward directed from 10:00 to 16:00 UT on 2 August, for which Asia and Pacific regions were on night-side and dawn-side, respec- tively whereas Africa and America were on day-side and morning-side. During this period an enhancement in REC and vTEC occurred only in

Hence, strong energy inputs on 3 August resulted in the equator-ward flowing thermospheric winds and eventually caused changes in thermospheric composition and westward disturbance dynamo electric fields (Blanc & Richmond, 1980; Fuller-Rowell et al., 1994; Prölss, 1995). The strong night-time plasma enhancement can be attributed to the combined effect of PPEF and DDEF: during the day-time, PPEF is eastward and DDEF is westward, whereas both are westward on night-side and have caused a significant enhancement in plasma density (Liu et al., 2014). The asymmetry in the thermospheric O/N₂ at mid-latitudes can be associated with seasonal variations. There is quiet time wind flowing from summer (northern hemisphere in this case) to winter (southern hemisphere) and strong energy inputs further enhances this quiet time flow. As a result, there is degradation (enhancement) in O/N₂ in the northern (southern) hemisphere. This decrease in O/N₂ has also caused negative storm effects in the northern hemisphere (Fuller-Rowell et al., 1996; Liu et al., 2012).

The oscillatory behavior of IMF *Bz* is minimized after 3 August and consequently penetration of PPEF has also been reduced as indicated by its magnetic signatures (Figure 7b). However, the disturbance in the thermospheric O/N₂ and resultant dynamo electric fields are observed till the end of 7 August 2020. Hence, the disturbed thermospheric winds can persist over several days and the ionospheric perturbations from 04 to 07 August can be associated to these physical drivers (Navarro & Fejer,  2019; Younas, Amory-Mazaudier, et al.,  2021). The comparison of thermospheric O/N₂ changes with the CTIPe indicates that the model responds remarkably well at low-latitudes, and we find same enhancement in thermospheric composition. However, at mid-latitudes model failed to reproduce the hemispheric asymmetry in O/N₂. A similar discrepancy has also been reported in an earlier study by Younas, Khan, et al. (2021). The said disagreement may be associated with the fact that CTIPe model could not adequately take into account the effects of thermospheric Joule heating and atmospheric expansion at mid-latitudes during the disturbed geomagnetic conditions. Notwithstanding the plethora of studies dedicated to storms, there is still the need for the modeling community to understand the changes in the geospace especially during the storms as driven by coronal holes. This multi-instrument analysis has helped us to isolate the main drivers of ionospheric changes during various phases of the minor storm driven by HSSWs. For example, the sole action of PPEF modulated the enhancement observed in the morning and day-side ionosphere during the period of southward IMF *Bz* on 2 August. The eastward DDEF at night resulted in plasma increase on 3 August while the disturbed thermospheric winds and composition change prevailed and were responsible for the changes and asymmetry observed in the ionosphere from 04 to 07 August. The fact that SYM-H only reached about −30 nT reinforces the significance of this study and underscores the importance of HSSWs driven storm to the modeling community.

We have further compared the longitudinal difference in the ionospheric disturbance dynamo (Diono) generated by a minor storm driven by HSSWS (Figure 8). Our findings revealed features of significant interest which can contribute to enhancing our understanding of the nature of the magnetic perturbations generated during such event. Figure 8 which displays the Wavelet Power Spectrum of Diono over the Asian (GUA and DLT) and Ameri- can (HUA) sectors, shows short term oscillations with periods from 2 to 4 hr on 2 to 4 August. Nava et al. (2016) had reported similar oscillations during the most intense storm of solar cycle 24, the St Patrick Storm of March

2015. These corresponding DP2 fluctuations which occurred during eastward PPEF enhanced the equatorial electric field over longitude −130° to −90°. Particularly, at about −100°, the percentage increase in EEF recorded by Swarm A and B during the PPEF reached 25%. Another salient feature from the spectrum of Diono is the period between 12 and 24 hr. The 24 hr period lasted from the 2 to 7 August in GUA, (5 days) and till 6 August at DLT (4 days) and 5 August at HUA (3 days). These diurnal periods are the signature of Ddyn related to DDEF. Fathy et al. (2014) had established that such diurnal oscillations occurred during the storm of 5 April 2010 associated with HSSWS following a CME (SYM-H = −80 nT on 6 April 2010). ##### 5. Conclusions

Understanding the ionospheric disturbances—due to its impact on modern space borne instruments—is becom- ing more significant to human society, for example, the accuracy of positioning and GNNS navigations, and the quality of ground (as well as satellite) communications are strongly affected by ionospheric conditions. In this regard, the variations of ionospheric electron density can play a major role and is seen to be strongly connected with the space weather conditions (Grandin et  al.,  2015), hence it is considered to be an important topic of research for the respective community. In this work, ionospheric response to high-speed solar wind—associated with coronal hole activity of August 2020—has been studied in detail. In this regard the roles of several physical drivers have also been identified.
